What Happens When the Engagement Begins

Every engagement begins the same way, by listening before acting. Chuck meets with leadership first, then with the team, to understand the current state from both perspectives. The gap between what leadership sees and what the team experiences is usually where the work begins.

From there, the focus turns to the data, the process, the customers, and the opportunities, building the structure and accountability that makes results repeatable. The current sales process, historical performance, and forecasting discipline are reviewed and rebuilt where needed.

Leadership stays informed throughout with clear, consistent reporting that connects commercial activity to business outcomes. Every engagement is different. The approach adapts to what the situation requires.

Not every organization needs a full-time sales executive. Many benefit from working with a leader who:
  • Has the experience creating what is needed
  • Will get to know the organization's culture, roll up their sleeves, and collaborate at all levels
  • Can support individuals in realizing their potential
  • Knows how to build upon the current sales process for improved sustainability that supports both the top and bottom lines
Chuck's approach is not to replace your team, and it is not to manage them from above. It is to become a trusted colleague who works alongside them, present, engaged, and invested in their success, while building the systems and disciplines that stay long after the engagement ends.

Questions Worth Asking.

Straightforward answers about what GENREV!™ is, who it serves, and how it works.

What is GENREV!™?

GENREV!™ is a commercial leadership resource for industrial B2B manufacturers and distributors under $50M in revenue. The work is built around three disciplines that drive profitable revenue growth: Forecasting, Strategy, and Performance Management. These are brought together through Sales Resource Management (SRM), a proactive operating discipline supported by a purpose-built platform and installed by the person who built it. Most organizations can find one of these. GENREV!™ brings all three.

Who is GENREV!™ designed for?

GENREV!™ is designed for B2B manufacturers and distributors that want to strengthen their sales effort, improve forecasting, drive margin performance, and build the organizational alignment that makes results repeatable. It is well suited for companies with an established customer base and a dedicated sales effort, whether a single contributor or a full team, that are looking for a practical, experienced approach to improving sales clarity, focus, and results. For very early-stage organizations with no sales history and purely opportunistic selling, simpler tools may be more appropriate during that initial phase.

What business challenges does GENREV!™ help address?

The challenges GENREV!™ addresses are common in manufacturing and distribution organizations of this size: sales forecasts that miss consistently, margins that erode without a clear explanation, a sales team that works hard but lacks structural focus, difficulty identifying where the best growth opportunities actually are, and leadership that lacks real visibility into what is driving or limiting performance. Sales is often the part of the business leaders feel least equipped to address. GENREV!™ provides the structure, accountability, and visibility to close that gap.

How is GENREV!™ different from hiring a full-time sales executive?

A full-time sales executive is a significant commitment in cost, in time, and in organizational change. GENREV!™ is available on a fractional, interim, or project basis, which means the organization gets experienced commercial leadership without the overhead of a permanent hire. It also brings a proven process and a purpose-built platform, installed by the person who built it, that stay with the organization after the engagement ends. The discipline that gets built is designed to be self-sustaining. The work is not dependent on one person remaining employed there.

How is working with GENREV!™ different from bringing in outside help?

GENREV!™ works alongside the leadership team and the sales team from day one, listening before acting, understanding the current state, and building from there. The goal is never to impose a generic framework or deliver a report and leave. The process and disciplines that are built during the engagement remain with the organization. What was built stays.

How does GENREV!™ support an organization's growth strategy?

By providing clear visibility into sales performance, future opportunities, and margin potential, GENREV!™ helps leadership teams align their sales effort with their actual growth objectives. Priorities, resources, and activities get focused where they will deliver the greatest business impact, not spread across whatever comes through the door.

How does GENREV!™ foster organizational alignment?

Sales performance rarely lives in one department. GENREV!™ provides a structured framework that helps sales, product management, engineering, operations, finance, and executive leadership operate from a shared understanding of priorities, progress, and financial impact. When everyone is working from the same picture, the conversation changes.

How does GENREV!™ help improve forecasting accuracy?

Forecasting improves when it is built on structure and accountability rather than optimism and habit. GENREV!™ brings a disciplined approach to understanding what is in the pipeline, what is real versus hopeful, and what the organization can actually count on. Customer-specific insights and profitability context give leadership a more honest picture of what to expect, and where to focus attention before the quarter closes.

How does GENREV!™ help manage sales opportunities?

GENREV!™ provides a clear, structured view of sales potential across key customers, industries, and account types. GENREV!™ is Sales Resource Management (SRM), which is a different category of work than CRM or BI. CRM records activity. BI reports on what happened. Business Development: More Than New Logos

April 28, 2026  |  Chuck Kitchen, Founder GENREV!™
One-Page Reference Guide
Business Development Scenarios with Reality Checks and Decision Considerations
↓ Download PDF

Business development is more than new logos. It is often treated as if it isn’t.

Growth comes from recognizing opportunities across existing customers, new customers, current capabilities, and new capabilities.

This is most immediately applicable to organizations with an existing customer base, but the same discipline applies to those building one.

Some opportunities are overlooked. Some are pursued without structure. Some are chased even when they do not meet the organization’s own criteria.

Across different environments, from strong brands with full offerings to fragmented, low-cost markets, these patterns consistently show up:

  • The easiest and most profitable new business often comes from existing customers, including referrals and introductions into similar opportunities
  • Without perceived value or differentiation, the conversation moves to price
  • Not every opportunity is worth pursuing. Clear go/no-go discipline matters
  • Every initiative requires a plan, a way to measure progress, and the willingness to adjust or stop
  • Capabilities are often misunderstood. They include everything the organization can deliver: services, engineering, operations, financial flexibility, and other elements that create value for the customer

Where Growth Actually Comes From

Most organizations focus heavily on new customers while underestimating what already exists.

Lost accounts go untouched. Existing customers are underpenetrated. New opportunities surface but are not pursued with structure or alignment.

At the same time, organizations pursue new markets or new offerings without fully understanding:

  • Where they can win
  • How they differentiate
  • Whether the effort meets their own criteria

Some invest heavily without clarity. Others default to chasing volume where price becomes the only lever.

Rather than treating business development as a single effort, it helps to break it into two dimensions:

  • Customer: Existing vs. New
  • Capabilities: Current vs. New

This creates a set of distinct scenarios, each with different levels of risk, effort, and potential return. The one-page reference above outlines these scenarios, along with the associated reality checks and decision considerations.

Guiding Principles

  • Start with existing customers. This is typically the most efficient path to growth
  • Capabilities are more than products. Organizations that recognize this outperform those that do not
  • Without differentiation, price becomes the decision driver
  • Not all business is good business. Discipline in selection matters
  • Strategy and tracking are required. Progress must be measured and adjusted

Final Thought

The strongest organizations are not the ones pursuing the most opportunities. They are the ones that understand where they can win, align their capabilities to real needs, and execute with discipline.

As soon as business is won, the focus should shift to how it will be defended. At the same time, alignment with existing and potential customers is critical to stay ahead of changing needs and industry direction.

The question is not where growth exists. The question is where your organization is choosing to focus.
