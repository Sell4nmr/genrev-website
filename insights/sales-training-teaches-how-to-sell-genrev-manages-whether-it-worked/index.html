Sales Training Teaches How to Sell. GENREV!™ Manages Whether It Worked.

June 2026  |  Chuck Kitchen, Founder GENREV!™

Sales training and Sales Resource Management solve different problems. Understanding the difference helps leaders invest in both, in the right order.

Organizations invest real money every year in sales training. Established methodologies teach salespeople how to qualify, build relationships, handle objections, and move a conversation toward commitment. That training has value. Skilled selling behavior matters.

But here is the gap that rarely gets addressed: six months after the training ends, can leadership say with confidence whether it changed the numbers?

In most organizations, the honest answer is no. The team learned new skills, but no system was put in place to measure whether those skills translated into a healthier pipeline, more reliable forecasts, or stronger margin. That is not a training failure. It is a missing management layer.

Two Different Jobs

Sales training answers how to sell. It is delivered to individuals, focused on technique, and measured by skill acquisition: role-play scores, certification, behavioral change in the room.

Sales Resource Management answers whether it is working. It is installed at the organizational level, focused on forecasting, strategy, and performance management, and measured by what actually shows up in revenue, margin, and pipeline quality over time.

One is a skill-building investment. The other is an operating discipline. Neither replaces the other.

Why the Gap Exists

Training methodologies are not designed to track customer-level forecasting accuracy, tie pipeline activity to margin outcomes, or give leadership an ongoing, structured view of whether the sales organization is actually performing. That was never their job. They teach the conversation. They do not manage the business around the conversation.

Without that management layer, even well-trained teams drift back into old habits, forecasts stay unreliable, and leadership has no structured way to know if the investment in training is paying off.

Where GENREV!™ Fits

GENREV!™ is not a competitor to sales training. It is built to pick up exactly where training leaves off, installing the forecasting discipline, strategic structure, and performance management rhythm that turns trained behavior into measurable, sustained results. Organizations that have invested in training get more from that investment when there is a system in place to manage what happens after the workshop ends.

The Practical Takeaway

If your team has been through sales training and leadership still cannot say with confidence whether the pipeline is real or the forecast is reliable, that is not a reason to abandon the training. It is a signal that a management system is missing. Training changes behavior. GENREV!™ manages the results.
